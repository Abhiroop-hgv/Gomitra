Resting more than usual, and eating less than usual (starter note, needs review by a veterinarian)

A healthy cow lies down for roughly half of the day to rest and chew the cud. A cow that suddenly lies down much more than she normally does, or that eats and chews much less than usual, may be unwell. These changes do not show what the problem is. They are an early reason to look at the cow.

What to check first:
- Is she eating and drinking? Check the feed trough and the water.
- Does she get up when you come near? A cow that stays down when approached needs a vet.
- Look at her feet and legs for swelling or heat, at the udder for swelling, and at the nose and mouth for sores or drooling.
- Feel for fever if you can. A cow that feels hot or shivers needs a vet.

What to do:
- Keep her on soft, dry, clean bedding and give clean water and fresh feed.
- Keep her apart from the herd if she looks very dull, or has sores in the mouth or on the feet, because some diseases spread.
- Call the vet the same day if she also eats less, has a fever, will not get up, or if several cows show the change.
- If many cows chew less at the same time, check the feed and water first: spoiled feed, a change of feed, or heat can cause this. Call the vet if it continues for a day.

Do not give medicines without the vet's advice.
