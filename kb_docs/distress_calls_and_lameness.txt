Loud calling, lameness and bloat: starter notes for farmers.
Loud or repeated calling: cows call loudly when they are hungry or thirsty, separated from their calf or herd, in heat, frightened, hurt or in pain, or when a calf is in trouble. First walk over and look: check water and feed, check the calf and the herd, look for injuries, swelling, a stuck leg or a tangled rope, and check for heat signs. If the calling continues, or the cow is also off feed, limping or straining, call the vet. Calling at night near calving time can mean the birth is starting, so watch for calving signs.
Limping or lameness: check the hoof for stones, cracks, cuts and swelling, and the leg for heat or swelling. Keep the animal on soft, dry ground and avoid long walks. Wash mud and dung off the hooves. If it does not improve in one to two days, or there is a bad smell, pus, fever or swelling, call the vet. Lameness together with mouth blisters can be foot-and-mouth disease, so separate the animal and call the vet at once.
Bloat: swelling of the left side of the belly behind the ribs, with discomfort, kicking at the belly, grunting and difficulty breathing. It is an emergency. Call the vet at once. Keep the animal standing and calm, do not force-feed or force-drink. It often follows eating a lot of fresh green fodder, wet clover or grain.
Eating less: a cow that eats or chews cud much less than usual may be sick. Note the change, check for fever and mouth problems, and call the vet if it lasts more than a day.
