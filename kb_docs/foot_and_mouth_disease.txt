Foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) in cattle and buffalo: starter notes for farmers.
What it is: FMD is a very contagious viral disease of cloven-hoofed animals such as cattle, buffalo, sheep, goats and pigs. It spreads quickly by direct contact, by drooling and breath, and on people's clothes and boots, vehicles, equipment, feed, water and milk containers.
Signs: high fever, drooling, blisters or sores in the mouth, on the tongue and gums, and between or above the hooves, smacking of the lips, refusing feed, limping or unwillingness to walk, and a sharp fall in milk. Young calves can die suddenly.
What to do first: separate the sick animals at once from the rest of the herd, and keep them in a separate shed with their own feed, water and equipment. Do not move any animals in or out. Call the veterinarian the same day and tell the local animal husbandry office, because FMD must be reported to the authorities.
Hygiene: wash hands, boots and clothes after handling sick animals and handle sick animals last. Do not share buckets, tools or feed between sick and healthy animals. Keep visitors and vehicles away from the sick animals. Ask the vet about safe disinfection.
Care of the sick animal: soft green feed or soft mash, clean drinking water, and a dry clean floor with soft bedding help the animal eat and walk. Only the vet should decide medicines and how to treat the mouth and foot sores.
Vaccination: FMD vaccination is part of the national control programme and is repeated regularly, usually twice a year. Ask your veterinarian or the nearest government veterinary hospital for the schedule and the dates in your area.
Call the vet urgently if: a calf is weak or dying, many animals show signs, an animal cannot eat or drink, or the hooves are badly damaged.
