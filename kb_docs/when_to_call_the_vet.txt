When to call the veterinarian: starter notes for farmers.
Call the vet the same day if an animal has any of these: a fever or feels hot, stops eating or chewing cud for a day, drinks much less than usual, milk drops suddenly, lumps or sores on the skin, blisters in the mouth, difficulty breathing, swelling of the belly on the left side, a limp that does not improve in one to two days, bleeding that does not stop, discharge from the nose, eyes or vagina, or any calving problem.
Call the vet at once, as an emergency, if: the left side of the belly is swollen and tight (bloat), the animal is down and cannot get up, the animal has fits or falls, there is heavy bleeding, a calf is stuck during birth for more than an hour of hard straining, or several animals become sick together.
Contagious diseases: if more than one animal has fever, skin lumps, mouth blisters or lameness, separate the sick animals, keep people and equipment from moving between sick and healthy animals, and inform the vet and the animal husbandry office. Do not sell or move the animals.
Until the vet comes: keep the animal in shade with clean water, do not force-feed or force-drink, do not give human medicines or home remedies without asking the vet, and write down when the signs started, what the animal ate, and what you noticed. Photos help the vet.
GoMitra alerts are screening hints from a camera and microphone. They are not a diagnosis. A vet should always confirm.
